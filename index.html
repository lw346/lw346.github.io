Grid. Rows are 10 km tall (1/2001 of a meridian). Each row is split into as many cells as fit its circumference at 10 km, so every cell covers about 100 km². Each cell is evaluated at its centre. Zoom in to see cell boundaries.

Orbits. Spherical Earth (R = 6371 km), circular orbits, two-body motion with optional J2 drift of node and argument of latitude. At t = 0, Greenwich and plane 1's ascending node are both at 0° longitude.

Revisit. A cell has access while its centre is inside any satellite's footprint (minimum elevation, capped by maximum off-nadir angle if set). A revisit gap is the time between two accesses, measured to the nearest time step. Gaps before the first and after the last access are left out unless you tick the option above.
